Recent solar activity (context)

Honest scope: these are solar events (CMEs, flares) recorded by Sun-Earth-line spacecraft (SOHO, STEREO, GOES) via NASA's DONKI catalog. They are not measurements of radiation at Mars. No public feed measures live radiation on the Martian surface today; solar particle events take hours to days to reach Mars, and Mars is usually far from the Sun-Earth line. Treat this panel as "what is the Sun doing", not "is it safe to go outside".

What we actually measured on Mars

  • MSL Curiosity's RAD instrument (Gale Crater, 2012-2013): average dose equivalent on the surface 0.64 ± 0.12 mSv/day (absorbed dose 0.21 ± 0.04 mGy/day) - roughly half the dose measured during the cruise to Mars, thanks to shielding by the atmosphere and the planet itself.
  • That is about 230 mSv per Earth year on the surface, before any habitat shielding - the central number for crew career-dose planning at any of the four sites.
  • One solar energetic particle (SEP) event added about 50 µSv in a day on top of the background - solar storms matter, but the everyday galactic cosmic ray background dominates.
  • Site effect: deeper sites (Gale -4.4 km, Arcadia -3 to -4 km, Valles floor -4 to -5 km) sit under more atmosphere = more shielding; Jezero at -2.6 km gets somewhat less.

Dust and other environmental hazards

  • Mars has regional and occasional planet-encircling dust storms (the 2018 global storm ended the Opportunity rover). They cut solar power and visibility for weeks - any site plan needs stored power or nuclear backup.
  • Fine dust is present at all four sites; NASA's ice map marks some dusty ground as unsafe to land on (it may not bear a lander's weight).
